## 2. Where the sources agree (the consensus core)

These points recur across ≥3 independent documents and form the non-negotiable spine of the redesign:

1. **A profile, never a single score.** Every high-authority source forbids collapsing the child into one number: ICF manual (no linear inference across domains; support needs in one area did not predict another), support-needs review (correlated-traits-correlated-methods fits; one-dimensional models fail), AAIDD (SIS-C = "profile of pattern and intensity"), Frontiers (two measures disagree per-child in both directions; "no single IQ number" is what parents consent to), Fletcher (ranges not thresholds; composites hide scatter). **IRIS will output domain-state profiles + confidence statements.**
2. **Function is measured in contexts, as habitual performance.** Ask "what does she usually do, where, with whom, with what help" over a defined recall window — not capacity in a clinical moment (Frontiers; Fletcher pp.19-20; ICF performance/capacity; UNICEF functional framing).
3. **The caregiver is the standard informant for daily-life functioning** — the child-aged reference instrument (SIS-C) is *by design* a structured interview with caregivers; when ability testing is suspect (age, language, severity), the field weights caregiver functional report MORE, never less (support-needs review; AAIDD catalogue; Fletcher pp.20-21; Frontiers). This legitimises IRIS's parent questionnaire as an instrument class — with the honest caveat that no source validates a *self-administered digital* version.
4. **Probe, don't checkbox.** The semi-structured interview format exists specifically to reduce responder bias by following up ("usually or only sometimes?"). IRIS implements conditional follow-ups per answer. (Frontiers; Fletcher; AAIDD DABS/SIS-C "structured interview"; support-needs review A4/CANS gate.)
5. **Separate "cannot" from "has not had the chance" from "will not".** ICF prints `.99 not applicable` precisely for the never-given-opportunity case; std-accom §4 turns it into conditions-of-display; the support-needs review condemns forced ratings of activities "not part of a person's life"; Fletcher warns low scores may reflect access skills/opportunity, not ability. **IRIS items get an explicit opportunity option, and it is excluded from difficulty counts.**
6. **Needs that are already met are not learning levers; unmet needs are.** CANDID's met/unmet parallel (sole instrument to do it) → IRIS personalisation keys off *unmet* support needs. (Support-needs review A5.)
7. **Type / frequency / time-of-support as three ordinal qualifiers** is the attested child-aged format (SIS/SIS-C triplet, 0–4). IRIS borrows the *triplet shape* at 5 levels, adapted to parent-comprehensible Arabic anchors. (Support-needs review; AAIDD.)
8. **Ask about activities, not body functions; ask about function with the usual aid.** ICF: intellectual functions cannot be observed directly — infer from the activity; WG: "even if wearing glasses / hearing aid"; UNICEF repeats it. **Parents never rate "attention" or "intelligence"; they rate observable doing.**
9. **The output is a plan.** AAIDD: assessment → support needs → plan (a concrete weekly schedule with settings/activities/supports) → outcome review; Schalock: PSPs jointly with the family; Fletcher: "move assessment from eligibility to treatment". **IRIS's questionnaire must terminate in an actionable weekly learning arrangement + what to recheck when, not a verdict card.**
10. **Re-measure on transitions, not calendars — and expect reported needs to drift down with age.** (Support-needs review A8: 5-10 vs 11-16 cohorts; AAIDD Annual Review Protocol; ICF fluidity.) A downward drift in reported needs is *normative*, not proof of learning; growth must be shown as within-child curves.
11. **Discrepancy between informants/sources is a finding, not noise.** Parent-vs-teacher agreement bands are wide (SIS interrater 0.30–0.98); averaging destroys exactly the unreliable component (support-needs review A7). IRIS reports disagreement explicitly.
12. **Language is a first-class confound to record before interpreting.** Lack of proficiency in the language of instruction is an exclusionary factor in every system; the field has "no clear criteria" separating difficulty from language exposure (Fletcher p.15); the WG item says "your usual customary language". For IRIS this means: record home language/dialect, school language, exposure history, and NEVER let a one-language reading weakness stand alone. MSA-vs-dialect diglossia handling is our extrapolation — the sources offer only the analogy, said plainly.
13. **Behaviour/emotional and medical support needs are separate named categories** (SIS/SIS-C "exceptional behavioural/medical support needs"; UNICEF psychosocial; AAIDD trauma-informed framing) — collected as support conditions, never scored as compliance or conduct.
14. **Play and participation are domains, not decoration.** WG Child Functioning includes *playing* and *relationships*; ICF names "playing in the school yard" as a life situation and says self-report is most meaningful for recreation; DS review shows attention/engagement are the practical bottleneck. IRIS's ladder and child mode already lean on play — the profile must capture it.
15. **Predictability, effort-reinforcement, untimed generous windows, and response-mode flexibility are assessment design requirements for THIS population** (std-accom; the strongest single meta-analytic anchor being motivation accommodations). The ladder implements them; the questionnaire implements the parent analogues (plain language, audio, one item per screen, autosave).

## 3. Where the sources conflict, and how IRIS resolves it

| Tension | Sources | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| "Adult-delivered beats computer" vs building an app | DS-2025 (flagship finding, n=2 computerised studies only) vs Frontiers (remote/self-admin "recommended", "more amenable to online studies") | Position IRIS as a **parent-mediated platform**: app-assisted, adult-present by default; the DS contrast is weak ("hypothesis-generating") but the direction is consistent — IRIS prompts co-play, gives the adult a role, and never markets self-directed screen time as equivalent therapy |
| Drill games for letter-sounds | Whole IRIS child mode is game-shaped vs DS-2025: pure phoneme-grapheme drill (GraphoGame) = null group results; holistic programs worked | Keep phonics work but always pair it with meaningful words/reading context in the plan; never ship a drill-only recommendation |
| 4-level vs 5-level response scales | WG short set uses 4 ("no/some/a lot difficulty/unable") while the attested support-needs triplet is 5 (0–4), and the review flags "never→always 4-level" as unattested | **Ability items: WG-style 4-anchor (+opportunity option). Support-intensity items: 5-level triplet.** Honest provenance stated; never call them "ICF qualifiers" |
| Screening short forms: legitimate? | Fletcher: short forms NEVER for level claims vs Frontiers/std-accom: brief repeated CBM-style probes are the best-evidenced method | IRIS: short measures are legitimate **for instruction-tuning (slope)** and descriptive profiles only; every output refuses level/severity/eligibility vocabulary (already the engine's doctrine) |
| Syndrome-informed priors | DS-2025/UNICEF say phenotype guides the questions; the whole pack (Fletcher, Schalock, audit doctrine) forbids diagnosis→pedagogy determinism | Priors become **hypotheses to confirm**: condition field may pre-set accommodation defaults (the single sanctioned touchpoint) and must never set ability expectations, entry points, or module order — this directly targets audit weakness W8 |
| Parent report bias vs parent authority | Frontiers/std-accom note interpretation bias; SIS-C/Fletcher/UNICEF make caregivers the primary informant | Keep both: structured probes with anchoring + examples, *and* cross-check against the child ladder, reporting divergence — never silently averaging |
| ICF framing vs non-diagnostic product | ICF p.6: "a health condition… is always understood to be present" vs IRIS never diagnoses | IRIS's profile is a **behavioural-educational observation system**, described on its own terms; it may say "aligned with the ICF's activity-participation logic" but never "ICF-based/ICF-scored" |

## 4. The existing IRIS assessment — evaluated against the consensus

(From `EXISTING_ASSESSMENT.md`; W-numbers there.)

**Worth keeping — genuinely good architecture:**
- Diagnosis-blind engine enforced *structurally* (no condition parameter anywhere in scoring; source-scan tests), age can only shorten the probe list, `indicator:null` never coerced to 0, append-only records, deterministic engines with 168 passing tests, profile-not-verdict parent language with banned-term governance, and the adaptive probe itself (3-item visits, climb/down rules, budgets) which is a faithful implementation of Fletcher's CBM logic and matches UNICEF's "identify educational needs" step.
- The ladder is exactly the **"ask the child directly"** instrument UNICEF p.97a demands — the caregiver questionnaire is the missing half.

**Core research verdict:** the current system measures *learning performance only* (28 literacy/numeracy/listening strands) and then rolls up to just 3 caregiver-report domains. Against the consensus core, it misses the entire **functional-adaptive track** (communication, self-care, motor/access, relationships & play, health/safety & behaviour support, opportunity & language context, interests/reinforcers) — the very track the field says predicts what a child can do and where support pays off, and the track with **no floor effects**. Domains-missing list in the audit (fine motor, expressive language, pragmatics, adaptive behaviour, attention/memory *as measured*, behaviour) matches this diagnosis exactly.

**The 20 audit weaknesses, grouped — each has a home in the redesign:**
1. **Scoring honesty:** W1 (supported=0 and hints disabled → every indicator gets a free +20), W6 (HIGH confidence unreachable), W13 (interruption/response-time fields dead), W19 (tier granularity cosmetic in assessment path). → Ladder must record prompts/scaffolds, response latencies (data-quality only), and modality use.
2. **Data integrity:** W2 (startDailySession routes on legacy flag → duplicate full re-assessments, insert-only server), W3 (resume device-local only), W4 (silent hydration failure; stop-reasons lost on restore), W17/W18 (two record generations share one table; `domain_scores` actually holds strand scores). → Server-side drafts with status `IN_PROGRESS`, idempotency keys, `assessment_kind` filtering per consumer, hydration errors surfaced with retry.
3. **Child experience vs this population's evidence (std-accom):** no progress predictability, no breaks, no fatigue guard (W14), invisible truncation penalises confidence (W4). → Child-visible "what's done / what's left", break button, session caps, "we couldn't measure this today" path replacing silent stops.
4. **Personalisation wiring:** the ladder's output barely reaches the app. W8 (legacy condition-driven moduleOrder still steers the child home — the forbidden diagnosis→pedagogy coupling), W7 (rung-biased priority list can ignore high-rung weaknesses), curriculum strands ↔ 14 legacy modules have no mapping, `startDailySession` routes on a legacy flag (W2). → Single sanctioned path: caregiver profile + ladder results → learningPriorities + access settings → plan & module order; condition field only for accommodation defaults.
5. **Parent output quality:** W11 (≈19/28 strands show generic watch-for text incl. an English sentence leaked into Arabic), W10 (unreviewed content presented as fact; validation status hidden from parents), W5 (no per-item transparency — "why ب 61?" unanswerable), W12 (unverified parent hits a dead-end mid-onboarding). → Real watch-for strings per new domain, honest validation-status surface, per-item result log, gate order fixed.
6. **Governance residues:** W9 (IQ-shaped fields still live client-side; legacy dashboard renders `assessmentResult.level`), W20 (aliased imports/multi-site mounts make the gate fragile). → Delete or quarantine IQ-shaped state; single guard component.

**What the research adds that did not exist at all:** the caregiver questionnaire (instrument class of SIS-C/DABS/Vineland family, adapted and self-authored), the two-track profile with divergence reporting, opportunity/met-unmet logic, context block (language exposure, school, routine, respondent identity, preferred formats), interests→reinforcers pipeline, accommodation ledger per observation, planned re-assessment cadence, and the "plan, not verdict" output contract.

## 5. Honest limits of the whole pack (must appear in product disclosures)

- **No source validates a self-administered digital Arabic parent questionnaire.** Every sanctioned format here is interviewer-administered; digital delivery is explicitly flagged unevidenced (support-needs review A11) or "more research needed" (std-accom). IRIS ships adapted formats and must pilot locally: content review by qualified professionals, internal consistency per domain, same-informant test-retest at 2–4 weeks, parent-vs-teacher agreement on a subset, interviewer-vs-self mode equivalence.
- **Nothing below age 5 is evidenced** anywhere except WG-CFM's lower bound of 2 for *screening*; 4–10 sits in a thin zone; the 4-year-old band is IRIS frontier territory (AAIDD support-needs starts at 5).
- **Zero Arabic/MENA psychometric content in all 9 documents.** Localisation = our construction; cognitive-test artifacts (Flynn correction, percentile norms) are explicitly NOT transplanted.
- **All item wording is IRIS-authored.** The review publishes no items; copyrighted instruments are not paraphrased; UNICEF's "ICF-based ≠ medical diagnosis" and WG domain grammar are standards, not items.
- **The evidence base skews WEIRD, 1:1-delivered, and under-represents higher support needs** — the children IRIS may serve most. Ceiling effects compress exactly where these profiles matter; IRIS's ladder floor must keep discriminating (std-accom §21).
- **Two sources are weaker than their reputations:** the AAIDD file is a sales catalogue; the ICF manual is an unfinalised draft. Both were used only for what they literally contain, with external corroboration labelled as such.
- **IRIS's own outcome claims stay at "descriptive/theoretical"** until its pilot data exist. The product promise is *personalisation informed by research*, not *improved outcomes*.

---

## 6. Bottom line for the framework

The redesign keeps the child-facing ladder (fixed), adds a caregiver functional-support profile (new, adapted from the SIS-C/DABS/Vineland/WG/ICF grammar), joins them into a two-track profile that never collapses to a number, and wires both into one sanctioned personalisation path ending in a weekly plan — all worded in simple Arabic-first, person-first language, with confidence, opportunity flags, accommodation records, and re-measurement built in.
